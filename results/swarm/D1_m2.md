# D1 — Redistribution algebra

Let $H$ be finite-dimensional over $\mathbb C$ (the same arguments work over $\mathbb R$, with adjoint replaced by transpose). Write $X^*$ for the adjoint of $X$, and use
\[
\|X\|_F^2=\operatorname{Tr}(X^*X)=\operatorname{Tr}(XX^*).
\]
I use finite-dimensional trace cyclicity, $\operatorname{Tr}(XY)=\operatorname{Tr}(YX)$.

Let $P=P^*=P^2$ and $Q=I-P$. Then
\[
Q^*=Q,\qquad Q^2=Q,\qquad PQ=QP=0,\qquad P+Q=I. \tag{1}
\]
For any linear operator $\Omega$ on $H$, define
\[
F(\Omega,P)=P\Omega Q+Q\Omega P.
\]

## (i) The redistribution operator is traceless

By (1) and trace cyclicity,
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(P\Omega Q)=\operatorname{Tr}(QP\Omega)=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(Q\Omega P)=\operatorname{Tr}(PQ\Omega)=0.
\]
Adding the two equalities gives
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Tr}F(\Omega,P)=0.}
\]
No symmetry or normality assumption on $\Omega$ is needed.

## (ii) Vanishing redistribution, commutation, and block diagonalisation

Put
\[
A=P\Omega Q,\qquad B=Q\Omega P.
\]
Expanding $\Omega=(P+Q)\Omega(P+Q)$ yields
\[
\Omega=P\Omega P+A+B+Q\Omega Q. \tag{2}
\]
Also,
\[
\Omega P=P\Omega P+B,\qquad P\Omega=P\Omega P+A,
\]
so
\[
F=A+B,\qquad [\Omega,P]:=\Omega P-P\Omega=B-A. \tag{3}
\]
The orthogonal relations (1) imply
\[
A=PFQ,\qquad B=QFP,\qquad A=-P[\Omega,P]Q,\qquad B=Q[\Omega,P]P. \tag{4}
\]
For example, $PFQ=P^2\Omega Q^2+PQ\Omega PQ=A$; the other three calculations are identical applications of (1).

Now consider the following three statements:

1. $F=0$;
2. $[\Omega,P]=0$;
3. $\Omega=P\Omega P+Q\Omega Q$.

If $F=0$, then (4) gives $A=B=0$, and (2) gives statement 3. Statement 3 says that $A=B=0$, so (3) gives $[\Omega,P]=0$; thus statement 3 implies statement 2. If $[\Omega,P]=0$, then (4) gives $A=B=0$, hence $F=A+B=0$. Finally, statement 3 immediately implies $F=0$ by the definition of $F$. Therefore
\[
\boxed{F=0\iff[\Omega,P]=0\iff\Omega=P\Omega P+Q\Omega Q.}
\]
In particular, this proves the requested equivalences for self-adjoint $\Omega$. The proof shows the slightly stronger fact that self-adjointness is not needed for this part.

## (iii) Frobenius-norm and trace identities

Now assume $\Omega^*=\Omega$. In the notation above, $B=A^*$, and consequently
\[
F=A+A^*,\qquad [\Omega,P]=A^*-A,\qquad F^*=F. \tag{5}
\]
Moreover, $QP=0$ and $PQ=0$ give
\[
A^2=P\Omega QP\Omega Q=0,
\qquad (A^*)^2=Q\Omega PQ\Omega P=0. \tag{6}
\]
Thus
\[
F^2=AA^*+A^*A,
\qquad
[\Omega,P]^2=-AA^*-A^*A. \tag{7}
\]
Taking traces and using $\operatorname{Tr}(AA^*)=\operatorname{Tr}(A^*A)=\|A\|_F^2$ yields
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(F^2)=2\|A\|_F^2,
\qquad
-\operatorname{Tr}([\Omega,P]^2)=2\|A\|_F^2. \tag{8}
\]
Since $F^*=F$, $\|F\|_F^2=\operatorname{Tr}(F^*F)=\operatorname{Tr}(F^2)$. On the other hand,
\[
[\Omega,P]^*=P\Omega-\Omega P=-[\Omega,P],
\]
so $\|[\Omega,P]\|_F^2=-\operatorname{Tr}([\Omega,P]^2)$. Combining these equalities with (8) gives the **corrected identity**
\[
\boxed{\|F\|_F^2=2\|P\Omega Q\|_F^2
=\|[\Omega,P]\|_F^2.}
\]
Thus the factor $\tfrac12$ attached to $\|[\Omega,P]\|_F^2$ in the proposed statement is erroneous. An immediate counterexample to that factor is given below in the correctness note.

For the remaining identity, use $P^2=P$, $Q^2=Q$, and trace cyclicity:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tr}(P\Omega Q\Omega)
&=\operatorname{Tr}(P^2\Omega Q\Omega)\\
&=\operatorname{Tr}(P\Omega Q\Omega P)\\
&=\operatorname{Tr}(P\Omega Q^2\Omega P)\\
&=\operatorname{Tr}\big((P\Omega Q)(Q\Omega P)\big)\\
&=\operatorname{Tr}(AA^*)=\|A\|_F^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Tr}(P\Omega Q\Omega)=\|P\Omega Q\|_F^2\geq0.}
\]
In particular, the trace on the left is automatically real.

## (iv) A non-normal example with $F\ne0$ and $\operatorname{Tr}F=0$

On $\mathbb C^2$ (or $\mathbb R^2$), take
\[
P=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \Omega=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct multiplication gives
\[
P\Omega Q=\Omega,\qquad Q\Omega P=0,
\qquad F=\Omega\ne0,
\qquad \operatorname{Tr}F=0.
\]
Furthermore,
\[
\Omega\Omega^*=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\Omega^*\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
so $\Omega\Omega^*\ne\Omega^*\Omega$. Thus this example has every property requested in (iv).

## Correctness note and numerical check

The second equality in the proposed part (iii) is **false as written**: it should not contain the factor $\tfrac12$. For a very small self-adjoint counterexample, use the same $P$ as in part (iv) but take
\[
\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then
\[
F=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
[\Omega,P]=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
P\Omega Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\|F\|_F^2=2,\qquad 2\|P\Omega Q\|_F^2=2,
\qquad \|[\Omega,P]\|_F^2=2,
\]
whereas $\tfrac12\|[\Omega,P]\|_F^2=1\ne2$. The corrected statement, proved above, is
\[
\boxed{\|F\|_F^2=2\|P\Omega Q\|_F^2=\|[\Omega,P]\|_F^2.}
\]
All other assertions in D1 are correct. Part (ii) in fact holds for every linear $\Omega$, not just a self-adjoint one. The positivity assertion in (iii) is, as stated, a self-adjoint result: its proof uses $Q\Omega P=(P\Omega Q)^*$.

The accompanying deterministic NumPy script `check_D1_m2.py` tests the trace identity on a generic complex matrix, the corrected self-adjoint norm/trace identities and the three-way equivalence, the counterexample to the proposed half-factor, and the explicit non-normal example. Its numerical residual is printed together with PASS/FAIL.
