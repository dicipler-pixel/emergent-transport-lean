# D2 — Quantum geometric tensor from projectors

## Conventions and a necessary correction

Let $x=(x^1,\dots,x^m)$ belong to an open subset of $\mathbb R^m$, let
$D_\mu=\partial_\mu P$, and let $Q=I-P$. The tensor **as written in the
question** is denoted by
\[
 Q^\circ_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{Tr}(D_\mu D_\nu).
\]
It must not be confused with the standard projector formula for the quantum
geometric tensor, which has an additional factor $P$. Indeed, Theorem 1
below shows that the curvature assertion for $Q^\circ$ is false. The
corrected tensor is
\[
 \mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}:=\operatorname{Tr}(P D_\mu D_\nu).
\]
We use $V^*$ for the conjugate transpose. If $V$ is an $n\times k$
orthonormal frame ($V^*V=I_k$, $P=VV^*$), our Berry convention is
\[
 A_\mu=iV^*\partial_\mu V,\qquad
 F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu-i[A_\mu,A_\nu].
 \tag{1}
\]
Thus $A_\mu$ and $F_{\mu\nu}$ are Hermitian $k\times k$ matrices. Using the
opposite sign for the Berry connection reverses the signs in the curvature
formulas below.

## 1. What is true for the tensor exactly as written

**Theorem 1.** For a $C^1$ family of orthogonal projectors, $Q^\circ$ is
real, symmetric, and positive semidefinite. In particular it is Hermitian
and $\operatorname{Im}Q^\circ=0$.

*Proof.* Cyclicity of trace gives
$Q^\circ_{\nu\mu}=\operatorname{Tr}(D_\nu D_\mu)=Q^\circ_{\mu\nu}$.
Since $D_\mu^*=D_\mu$,
\[
 \overline{Q^\circ_{\mu\nu}}
 =\operatorname{Tr}((D_\mu D_\nu)^*)
 =\operatorname{Tr}(D_\nu D_\mu)=Q^\circ_{\mu\nu}.
\]
For $z\in\mathbb C^m$, put $B_z=\sum_\nu z_\nu D_\nu$. Then
\[
 z^*Q^\circ z
 =\sum_{\mu,\nu}\bar z_\mu z_\nu\operatorname{Tr}(D_\mu D_\nu)
 =\operatorname{Tr}(B_z^*B_z)=\|B_z\|_F^2\ge0.
\]
This proves every assertion. $\square$

Consequently, no generally nonzero Berry curvature can equal
$\operatorname{Im}Q^\circ$. For an explicit counterexample, take the
spin-$1/2$ coherent-state projector $P=vv^*$ on $\mathbb C^2$, where
\[
 v(\theta,\phi)=(\cos(\theta/2),e^{i\phi}\sin(\theta/2))^T,
 \quad 0<\theta<\pi.
\]
Direct differentiation gives
\[
 Q^\circ_{\theta\theta}=\tfrac12,\qquad
 Q^\circ_{\phi\phi}=\tfrac12\sin^2\theta,\qquad
 Q^\circ_{\theta\phi}=Q^\circ_{\phi\theta}=0,
\]
whereas (1) gives
\[
 A_\theta=0,\qquad A_\phi=-\sin^2(\theta/2),\qquad
 F_{\theta\phi}=-\tfrac12\sin\theta.
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus $\operatorname{Im}Q^\circ_{\theta\phi}=0$, but the Berry curvature is
nonzero away from the poles. Hence **D2 is false as written**; the following
is the corrected statement.

## 2. The corrected QGT and its metric

Locally there is a $C^1$ orthonormal frame $V$ with $P=VV^*$. For
completeness, one construction is to fix an orthonormal basis $V_0$ of the
range at $x_0$, set $\widetilde V(x)=P(x)V_0$, and, near $x_0$, use
$V=\widetilde V(\widetilde V^*\widetilde V)^{-1/2}$.

**Theorem 2.** For an orthogonal-projector family, $\mathcal Q$ is Hermitian
and positive semidefinite (hence $\operatorname{Re}\mathcal Q$ is positive
semidefinite), and
\[
 Q^\circ_{\mu\nu}=2\operatorname{Re}\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}. \tag{3}
\]
If $W_\mu=Q\partial_\mu V$, then
\[
 \mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{Tr}(W_\mu^*W_\nu)
 =\operatorname{Tr}(\partial_\mu V^*\,Q\,\partial_\nu V). \tag{4}
\]

*Proof.* Differentiating $P^2=P$ and $Q^2=Q$ gives $PD_\mu P=0$ and
$QD_\mu Q=0$. Therefore $D_\mu=PD_\mu Q+QD_\mu P$. Also
\[
 \overline{\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}}
 =\operatorname{Tr}(D_\nu D_\mu P)
 =\operatorname{Tr}(P D_\nu D_\mu)=\mathcal Q_{\nu\mu},
\]
so $\mathcal Q$ is Hermitian. The off-diagonal identities yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 Q^\circ_{\mu\nu}
 &=\operatorname{Tr}(PD_\mu QD_\nu P)
   +\operatorname{Tr}(QD_\mu PD_\nu Q)\\
 &=\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}+\mathcal Q_{\nu\mu}
 =2\operatorname{Re}\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For (4), use $PD_\mu Q=V\partial_\mu V^*Q$ and
$QD_\nu P=Q\partial_\nu V V^*$, together with $V^*V=I_k$ and cyclicity:
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(PD_\mu QD_\nu P)
 =\operatorname{Tr}(\partial_\mu V^*Q\partial_\nu V).
\]
Finally, for $z\in\mathbb C^m$ and $W_z=\sum_\nu z_\nu W_\nu$,
\[
 z^*\mathcal Qz=\operatorname{Tr}(W_z^*W_z)\ge0.
\]
Theorem 1 and (3) independently give positivity of the real part. $\square$

Thus the standard quantum metric is
$g_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{Re}\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}
=\tfrac12Q^\circ_{\mu\nu}$.

## 3. Exact curvature identity and rank dependence

**Theorem 3.** With convention (1),
\[
 \operatorname{tr}F_{\mu\nu}
 =i\operatorname{Tr}(P[D_\mu,D_\nu])
 =i(\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}-\mathcal Q_{\nu\mu})
 =-2\operatorname{Im}\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}. \tag{5}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 \mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}
 -\frac{i}{2}\operatorname{tr}F_{\mu\nu}. \tag{6}
\]

*Proof.* Define $B_\mu=V^*\partial_\mu V$. Differentiating $V^*V=I$ shows
$B_\mu^*=-B_\mu$, and
$\partial_\mu V=VB_\mu+W_\mu$. A direct expansion, in which all second
derivatives cancel, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 K_{\mu\nu}:={}&\partial_\mu B_\nu-\partial_\nu B_\mu+[B_\mu,B_\nu]\\
 ={}&(\partial_\mu V^*\partial_\nu V-\partial_\nu V^*\partial_\mu V)
      +[B_\mu,B_\nu]\\
 ={}&W_\mu^*W_\nu-W_\nu^*W_\mu.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because $A_\mu=iB_\mu$, (1) says $F_{\mu\nu}=iK_{\mu\nu}$. Taking traces
and using (4) proves the first and third expressions in (5). The middle
expression follows immediately from the definition of $\mathcal Q$.
Finally, $\mathcal Q_{\nu\mu}=\overline{\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}}$, so
$i(\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}-\mathcal Q_{\nu\mu})
=-2\operatorname{Im}\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}$. $\square$

For $k=1$, $A$ and $F$ are scalars and this is the usual rank-one identity.
For $k>1$, scalar $\mathcal Q$ determines **only the trace** of the
non-Abelian curvature, not its individual matrix entries or the separate
curvatures of a splitting into bands. There is no extra factor $k$ in (5).
For example, a direct sum of $k$ copies of one line family multiplies both
$\mathcal Q$ and $\operatorname{tr}F$ by $k$; dividing both by $k$ gives the
per-band normalization.

For the spin example,
\[
 \mathcal Q_{\theta\theta}=\tfrac14,\quad
 \mathcal Q_{\phi\phi}=\tfrac14\sin^2\theta,\quad
 \mathcal Q_{\theta\phi}=\frac{i}{4}\sin\theta,
\]
in agreement with (2) and (5).

## 4. Closedness

Use the matrix-valued forms
\[
 \mathcal A=A_\mu dx^\mu,\qquad
 \mathcal F_2=\tfrac12F_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu\wedge dx^\nu
 =d\mathcal A-i\mathcal A\wedge\mathcal A.
\]
On any local frame domain, $\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal A\wedge\mathcal A)=0$,
so
\[
 \operatorname{tr}\mathcal F_2=d(\operatorname{tr}\mathcal A). \tag{7}
\]
If $P$ is $C^2$, the frame constructed above is $C^2$, so $\mathcal A$ is
$C^1$. Equality of mixed partials gives
\[
 d(\operatorname{tr}\mathcal F_2)=d^2(\operatorname{tr}\mathcal A)=0
\]
initially in the weak exterior-derivative sense. This is also a classical
pointwise identity under the stated $C^2$ hypothesis: the middle expression
in (5) contains only $P$, its first derivatives, and products of first
derivatives, so $\operatorname{tr}F_{\mu\nu}$ is $C^1$. Hence the
coefficients of the exterior derivative in question are continuous, and a
distributionally zero continuous $3$-form is zero pointwise. These local
calculations prove global closedness of the gauge-invariant trace curvature
form. By (5), the form
$\tfrac12\operatorname{Im}\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu\wedge dx^\nu$ is also
closed and equals $-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\mathcal F_2$. For comparison,
$\operatorname{Im}Q^\circ=0$ is closed for the trivial reason that it is
identically zero.

## 5. Precisely which periods are integral

Let $E=\operatorname{im}P$, a complex rank-$k$ vector bundle over the
parameter space $X$. For a smooth map $u:\Sigma\to X$ from a **closed oriented
$2$-manifold** $\Sigma$, define the Berry Chern number and curvature period by
\[
 C_B(u):=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\Sigma u^*\operatorname{tr}\mathcal F_2.
\]
The transition between orthonormal frames is $V_b=V_aU_{ab}$ with
$U_{ab}\in U(k)$. On $\det E$, its unitary transition function is
$\det U_{ab}$, and (1) is a unitary connection on that determinant line.
With $B_\mu$ as above, $F$ is $i$ times the usual anti-Hermitian differential
geometric curvature. The Chern--Weil formula therefore gives, with the
sign convention (1),
\[
 \int_\Sigma u^*\operatorname{tr}\mathcal F_2
 =-2\pi\,\langle c_1(\det u^*E),[\Sigma]\rangle,
 \qquad C_B(u)\in\mathbb Z. \tag{8}
\]
(The Chern--Weil normalization follows by Stokes' theorem on a triangulation:
overlap differences of the local connections are logarithmic derivatives of
unit-modulus determinant transitions, and the cocycle condition makes the
sum of their phase changes $2\pi$ times an integer.) Additivity gives the
same statement for every integer $2$-cycle $z=\sum_j a_j u_j$. Rank enters
through $\det E$: a $k$-fold direct sum has $k$ times the determinant-line
Chern number and $k$ times the period.

Equation (5) also gives
$\int_\Sigma u^*\operatorname{Im}\mathcal Q_2=-\pi C_B(u)$.
There is no quantization assertion for a surface with boundary or for an
arbitrary real $2$-chain. Nor is a loop integral of the connection itself
necessarily in $2\pi\mathbb Z$: on the equator of the spin example,
$\oint A_\phi\,d\phi=-\pi$. Only the determinant-line holonomy is naturally
defined modulo $2\pi$; it is the **closed $2$-cycle curvature periods** in
(8) that are $2\pi$ times integers.

## 6. Exactly what fails for skew/Riesz projectors

For a general smooth idempotent, $D_\mu$ need not be self-adjoint. Cyclicity
still makes $Q^\circ$ symmetric, but reality, Hermiticity, positivity, (3),
(4), and the unitary $U(k)$ Berry interpretation all can fail. Since
$Q^\circ$ is symmetric, a nonzero $\operatorname{Im}Q^\circ$ is symmetric
and cannot itself be an antisymmetric curvature tensor.

Here are explicit $2\times2$ counterexamples, with $v,w$ real parameters near
zero. First,
\[
 P_R(v,w)=\frac1{1+vw}
 \begin{pmatrix}1&w\\v&vw\end{pmatrix}
\]
is idempotent and non-orthogonal. At $v=w=0$,
$D_v=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and
$D_w=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, giving
\[
 Q^\circ=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \mathcal Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus $Q^\circ$ is indefinite and $\mathcal Q$ is not Hermitian. Second,
\[
 P_C(v,w)=\frac1{1+ivw}
 \begin{pmatrix}1&iw\\v&ivw\end{pmatrix}
\]
is also a non-orthogonal idempotent, and at the origin
\[
 Q^\circ=\begin{pmatrix}0&i\\i&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
which is not Hermitian. Neither family has $P=VV^*$ for its defining
biorthogonal frame, so the orthonormal-frame proof cannot be applied.

It would be too strong to say that every trace-curvature closedness or Chern
integrality result fails. One can deliberately choose a **non-unitary**
connection on $\operatorname{im}P$ using biorthogonal frames; its trace
curvature still satisfies a local $dA$ formula, and Chern--Weil integrality
applies to that connection. What is lost is the claimed Hermitian/positive
QGT, the identification with ordinary unitary Berry curvature, and generally
the reality of the Berry phase; separate real and imaginary periods have no
such automatic quantization.
