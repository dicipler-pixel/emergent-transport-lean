# D6: integer winding, level gap, and spectral flow

## 1. Conventions and status of the proposed statement

Use
\[
\sigma _1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\sigma _2=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\sigma _3=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
\]
and identify $S^1=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$, oriented by increasing $t$.
For continuous real $a,b,c$, put
\[
H=a\sigma _1+b\sigma _2+c\sigma _3
  =\begin{pmatrix}c&\bar z\\z&-c\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad z=a+ib,\qquad r=\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}.
\]
The reference level is a fixed real number $\mu$. “No eigenvalue crosses
$\mu$” means $\mu\notin\sigma(H(t))$ for **every** $t$.

The statement proposed in D6 is false without additional hypotheses, for
three distinct reasons.

1. A gap at $\mu=0$ does not imply that $z$ is nonzero, so its winding need
   not be defined: take $H\equiv\sigma _3$. It has spectrum $\{-1,1\}$ but
   $z\equiv0$.
2. Even if $z$ is nowhere zero, its winding is generally **not** the
   self-adjoint spectral flow through $\mu$. For
   \[
   H_0(t)=\cos t\,\sigma _1+\sin t\,\sigma _2,
   \]
   the spectrum is constantly $\{-1,1\}$, $w(z)=1$, and
   $\operatorname{sf}_0(H_0)=0$. This counterexample also belongs to the
   special case $c\equiv0$.
3. Winding is a complete homotopy invariant only after restricting to loops
   for which $z$ remains nonzero. It is not invariant under arbitrary
   homotopies through the full class gapped at the zero level, where $z$ may
   vanish.
   Moreover, for $\mu\ne0$, the side of the forbidden radius $r=|\mu|$ is an
   additional locally constant invariant.

The corrected results are proved below.

## 2. Spectrum and the no-crossing condition

The Pauli relations $\sigma _j^2=I$ and
$\sigma _j\sigma _k+\sigma _k\sigma _j=0$ for $j\ne k$ give
\[
H(t)^2=r(t)^2I.                                    \tag{2.1}
\]
Consequently, when $r>0$, $H$ has the two eigenvalues $r$ and $-r$ (their
multiplicities are one because $\operatorname{Tr}H=0$); when $r=0$, $H=0$.
Thus
\[
\sigma(H(t))=\{-r(t),r(t)\},
\]
and the no-crossing hypothesis is exactly
\[
r(t)\ne R\quad(t\in S^1),\qquad R:=|\mu|.          \tag{2.2}
\]
At $\mu=0$ this is simply $r(t)>0$. In particular, it does not exclude
$a=b=0$ and $c\ne0$.

If $\mu\ne0$, continuity of $r$ and (2.2), together with the intermediate
value theorem, imply that exactly one of the following holds throughout the
loop:
\[
r(t)>R\quad\text{for all }t,\qquad\text{or}\qquad
0\le r(t)<R\quad\text{for all }t.                    \tag{2.3}
\]
Call this dichotomy the **radius region**.


## 3. Continuous winding: definition, integrality, and homotopy invariance

Assume in this section that $z:S^1\to\mathbb C^*$ is continuous. Write
$u=z/|z|$. We claim that there is a continuous function
$\vartheta:[0,2\pi]\to\mathbb R$ such that
\[
u(t)=e^{i\vartheta(t)},\qquad t\in[0,2\pi].           \tag{3.1}
\]
Here and below, a function on $S^1$ is identified with its $2\pi$-periodic
representative on $[0,2\pi]$.

For completeness, here is a construction of the lift. By uniform continuity,
choose $\delta>0$ such that $|u(t)-u(s)|<1$ whenever $|t-s|<\delta$.
On any interval $J$ of length less than $\delta$, based at $s\in J$,
\[
\frac{u(t)}{u(s)}\in\{q\in\mathbb C:|q-1|<1\},
\]
a set contained in the open right half-plane. If $\vartheta(s)$ is any choice
for which $e^{i\vartheta(s)}=u(s)$, then
\[
\vartheta(t)=\vartheta(s)+\operatorname{Arg}\frac{u(t)}{u(s)}
\]
gives the unique continuous lift on $J$ agreeing with the choice at $s$;
$\operatorname{Arg}$ is the principal argument. Partition $[0,2\pi]$ into
such intervals and glue consecutive lifts, shifting the next one by an
integer multiple of $2\pi$ so that it agrees at the common endpoint. This
proves (3.1).

Define the **winding number**
\[
w(z):=\frac{\vartheta(2\pi)-\vartheta(0)}{2\pi}.     \tag{3.2}
\]
Since $u(2\pi)=u(0)$, the endpoint phases differ by $2\pi k$ for some
$k\in\mathbb Z$. Hence $w(z)=k\in\mathbb Z$. Any other continuous lift is
$\vartheta+2\pi\ell$ with constant $\ell\in\mathbb Z$ (two lifts have
$2\pi$-integer-valued continuous difference), so (3.2) is independent of the
lift. More generally,
\[
z=|z|e^{i\vartheta},\qquad
w(z)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_0^{2\pi}d\log z                \tag{3.3}
\]
when $z$ is $C^1$; definition (3.2) needs no differentiability.

The same construction on compact rectangles gives a jointly continuous lift
for a homotopy $z_s:S^1\to\mathbb C^*$ continuous in $s$. Therefore
$w(z_s)$ is a continuous integer and is constant. In particular, winding is
locally constant: if $\delta=\min_{S^1}|z|>0$ and
$\|z'-z\|_\infty<\delta$, then $(1-s)z+sz'$ never vanishes, so
$w(z')=w(z)$.

For a $C^1$ family avoiding $\mu$ at every parameter, spectral flow is
defined as the signed number of eigenvalue crossings of $\mu$, with upward
crossings counted positively. The same net-crossing definition applies more
generally by continuous spectral-flow theory. Under the present hypothesis,
the crossing set is empty. Hence, without any differentiability assumption,
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{sf}_\mu(H)=0.}                    \tag{3.4}
\]
There is therefore no general equality between $\operatorname{sf}_\mu(H)$ and
$w(z)$: whenever the latter is nonzero, the proposed equality fails. This is
not a matter of fixing a sign or factor.

### The basic counterexample, explicitly

For $H_0(t)$ from Section 1, $z=e^{it}$, a phase lift is $\vartheta(t)=t$, and
\[
w(z)=1,\qquad \sigma(H_0(t))=\{-1,1\},\qquad
\operatorname{sf}_0(H_0)=0.                          \tag{3.5}
\]
Reversing the orientation of $t$ gives $w=-1$ and leaves the spectral flow
zero. Multiplying $H_0$ by any positive constant gives the same
counterexample at any $\mu$ different from the two constant eigenvalues.


## 4. The correct signed Berry-holonomy identity when $c\equiv0$

There is a useful projector/eigenvector interpretation, but it is a Berry
phase identity, not spectral flow. Suppose that $c\equiv0$ and that $z$ is
$C^1$. At $\mu=0$, the no-crossing hypothesis gives $\rho:=|z|>0$, so no
extra nonvanishing assumption is needed. With $\vartheta$ as in (3.1),
\[
H=\rho(\cos\vartheta\,\sigma _1+\sin\vartheta\,\sigma _2).
\]
A normalized negative-eigenvalue eigenvector is
\[
v_-(t)=\frac1{\sqrt2}
       \begin{pmatrix}1\\-e^{i\vartheta(t)}\end{pmatrix},
       \qquad Hv_-=-\rho v_-.                          \tag{4.1}
\]
Use the real Berry convention
\[
\mathcal A_-:=i\langle v_-,dv_-\rangle,
\]
where the inner product is linear in the second argument. Directly from
(4.1),
\[
\dot v_-=\frac1{\sqrt2}
 \begin{pmatrix}0\\-i\dot\vartheta e^{i\vartheta}\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \langle v_-,\dot v_-\rangle=\frac{i}{2}\dot\vartheta,
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_-=-\frac12\,d\vartheta.                    \tag{4.2}
\]
Thus the Berry phase $\gamma_-=\oint\mathcal A_-$ satisfies
\[
\boxed{\gamma_-=-\pi w(z)\pmod{2\pi}},\qquad
\boxed{\exp(i\gamma_-)=\exp(-i\pi w(z))=(-1)^{w(z)}}.       \tag{4.3}
\]
In the particular gauge (4.1), the integrated value is exactly $-\pi w(z)$.
A periodic change of gauge shifts the integrated Berry phase by an integer
multiple of $2\pi$ and leaves the holonomy $\exp(i\gamma_-)$ unchanged.
Using $-i\langle v,dv\rangle$ instead reverses the displayed sign. The radius
$\rho(t)$ does not enter (4.2).

Berry holonomy alone detects only $w\bmod2$. The full projector path carries
more information: in this chiral case its off-diagonal entry determines
$e^{i\vartheta(t)}$, so the full path also determines the integer $w$.

The formula does **not** extend unchanged to nonzero $c$. For example, keep
$z=e^{it}$ and let $c=C$ be constant. Put $r_C=\sqrt{1+C^2}$ and choose
$\beta\in(0,\pi)$ with $\cos\beta=C/r_C$. A direct substitution shows that
\[
v_{-,C}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\sin(\beta/2)\\
                         -e^{it}\cos(\beta/2)\end{pmatrix}
\]
is a negative-band eigenvector. The same calculation now gives
\[
\gamma_-(C)=-\pi\left(1+\frac{C}{r_C}\right)\pmod{2\pi},
\]
although $w(z)=1$ for every $C$. It reduces to (4.3) at $C=0$.

## 5. Precise “only invariant” statement

### 5.1 Reference level zero

Let
\[
\mathcal P_0=\{(a,b,c)\in C(S^1,\mathbb R^3):
                  z=a+ib\text{ is nowhere zero}\}.
\]
Every member of $\mathcal P_0$ is gapped at zero, because
$r\ge|z|>0$. Consider homotopies that remain in $\mathcal P_0$.

**Theorem.** $w(a+ib)$ is constant on homotopy classes in $\mathcal P_0$,
and two members of $\mathcal P_0$ with the same winding are homotopic within
$\mathcal P_0$. Thus $w$ is a complete invariant for these homotopies.

**Proof.** Constancy follows from Section 3. For completeness, first deform
$(a,b,c)$ to $(a,b,0)$ by
\[
(a,b,(1-s)c),\qquad0\le s\le1,
\]
which leaves $z$ unchanged. Then, with $\rho=|z|$, deform the chiral loop by
\[
\frac{(1-s)\rho+s}{\rho}(a,b),\qquad0\le s\le1.
\]
The new $z$ still never vanishes, and at $s=1$ it takes values in the unit
circle. It remains to homotope two unit-circle loops $u_0,u_1$ of the same
degree $w$. Choose continuous phase lifts $\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1$. Their
total phase changes are both $2\pi w$, so
\[
\vartheta_s=(1-s)\vartheta_0+s\vartheta_1,
\qquad u_s=e^{i\vartheta_s}
\]
has $u_s(2\pi)=u_s(0)$ for every $s$ and joins $u_0$ to $u_1$. This proves
both directions. $\square$

In the chiral subclass $c\equiv0$, this is precisely the familiar statement
that a continuous loop $S^1\to\mathbb C^*$ is classified by its degree.
Radial size is not an additional invariant, since it can be normalized while
preserving the gap. This is the rigorous sense in which the integer is the
only locally constant homotopy invariant.

The restriction to $z\ne0$ throughout the homotopy is essential. For example,
the degree-one equatorial loop can be deformed through matrices gapped at
zero to the constant matrix $\sigma _3$ by
\[
X_s(t)=\big(\sqrt{1-s}\cos t,\sqrt{1-s}\sin t,\sqrt{s}\big),
 \qquad0\le s\le1.                                  \tag{5.1}
\]
Indeed $|X_s|^2=1$ for every $s$, but at the endpoint $z=0$. A subsequent
rotation $(\sin q,0,\cos q)$, $0\le q\le\pi/2$, joins it to the constant
matrix $\sigma _1$, whose winding is zero. Therefore arbitrary homotopies in
the full class $\{r>0\}$ do not preserve winding.

### 5.2 General fixed reference level

For $\mu\ne0$, write $R=|\mu|$ and set
\[
\mathcal P_\mu=\{(a,b,c)\in C(S^1,\mathbb R^3):
 z\ne0\text{ and }r\ne R\text{ everywhere}\}.
\]
Then the pair
\[
\big(w(z),\eta\big),\qquad \eta=\operatorname{sgn}(r-R),
\]
is a complete invariant for homotopies within $\mathcal P_\mu$. We already
proved that $\eta$ is constant on each member; it cannot change along an
admissible homotopy without $r=R$ occurring.

For completeness in a fixed radius region, flatten and normalize as follows.
If $r<R$, first replace $c$ by $(1-s)c$; this preserves $r<R$. Then move
$\rho=|z|$ linearly to $R/2$, preserving $0<\rho<R$. The result is an
equatorial circle of radius $R/2$.

If $r>R$, first move $\rho$ linearly to $\max(\rho,2R)$; this can only
increase $r$. Next send $c$ to zero, which is now safe because $\rho\ge2R$.
Finally move $\rho$ linearly to $2R$. The result is an equatorial circle of
radius $2R$. In either region, phase interpolation exactly as in Section 5.1
classifies the remaining homotopy classes by $w$.

Consequently, if $\mu\ne0$, $w$ alone is not the only invariant: the constant
matrices $(R/2)\sigma _1$ and $2R\sigma _1$ both have $w=0$ but lie in
opposite radius regions and cannot be joined without an eigenvalue crossing
$\mu$.

## 6. Corrected compact statement

A concise replacement for D6 is:

> Let $H=a\sigma _1+b\sigma _2+c\sigma _3$ be continuous on the oriented
> circle and let $z=a+ib$. The eigenvalues are $\pm\sqrt{|z|^2+c^2}$. If
> $z$ is nowhere zero, then $w(z)\in\mathbb Z$; it is locally constant and
> complete for homotopies preserving this nonvanishing condition (with the
> radius region added when $\mu\ne0$). If a fixed level $\mu$ is never an
> eigenvalue, ordinary self-adjoint spectral flow through $\mu$ is zero, not
> $w(z)$. If moreover $c\equiv0$ and $\mu=0$, the zero gap is automatic,
> $w(z)$ is defined, and, for a $C^1$ family, the negative-band Berry
> convention $\mathcal A_-=i\langle v_-,dv_-\rangle$ gives
> $\gamma_-=-\pi w(z)\pmod{2\pi}$ and
> $\exp(i\gamma_-)=(-1)^{w(z)}$.
