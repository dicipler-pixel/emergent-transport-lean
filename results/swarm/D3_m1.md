# D3. Metric–curvature bound, with a necessary correction of definitions

## 1. The literal definition has no imaginary part

Throughout, derivatives are taken at a fixed point, finite-dimensional traces are used, and $^*$ means the conjugate transpose. Put
\[
  Q^{\rm lit}_{\mu\nu}:=\operatorname{Tr}(\partial_\mu P\,\partial_\nu P).
\]
Since $P=P^*$, both $A=\partial_\mu P$ and $B=\partial_\nu P$ are self-adjoint. Consequently
\[
 \overline{Q^{\rm lit}_{\mu\nu}}
 =\operatorname{Tr}((AB)^*)
 =\operatorname{Tr}(BA)
 =Q^{\rm lit}_{\nu\mu}
 =\operatorname{Tr}(AB)=Q^{\rm lit}_{\mu\nu}.
\]
The last equality is cyclicity of the trace. Hence **$Q^{\rm lit}$ is real and symmetric for every orthogonal projector, of every rank**. In fact
\[
 Q^{\rm lit}_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{Tr}(A^*B)
\]
is the (necessarily real) Hilbert–Schmidt inner product of two self-adjoint matrices.

Writing $g^{\rm lit}=\operatorname{Re}Q^{\rm lit}=Q^{\rm lit}$, we therefore have
\[
 \operatorname{Im}Q^{\rm lit}_{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad
 g^{\rm lit}_{\mu\mu}=\|\partial_\mu P\|_F^2.
\]
Thus the written inequality
$|\operatorname{Im}Q^{\rm lit}_{\mu\nu}|
 \le 2\sqrt{g^{\rm lit}_{\mu\mu}g^{\rm lit}_{\nu\nu}}$
holds, but only because its left side is zero. Equality with the factor $2$ holds exactly when
\[
 \sqrt{g^{\rm lit}_{\mu\mu}g^{\rm lit}_{\nu\nu}}=0,
\]
namely when $\partial_\mu P=0$ or $\partial_\nu P=0$. A constant family gives an equality example, but there is no nondegenerate equality example and no curvature information in this expression. Also, the optimal multiplying constant for the literally written expression is $0$, not $2$. In this sense the assertion that the written bound is sharp is false.

The remainder of this note proves the standard intended statement and identifies exactly where the factors $1$ and $2$ belong.

## 2. The standard projected QGT in an arbitrary orthonormal frame

Let $P(x)$ be a $C^1$ rank-$k$ orthogonal projector. Define the **projected quantum geometric tensor**
\[
 \boxed{\ \mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}
 :=\operatorname{Tr}(P\,\partial_\mu P\,\partial_\nu P)\ },\qquad
 g_{\mu\nu}:=\operatorname{Re}\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}.
\]
We use $\mathcal Q$ to distinguish it from $Q^{\rm lit}$.

A local orthonormal frame can be constructed without a genericity assumption: fix a matrix $U_0$ whose columns are an orthonormal basis of the range at $x_0$, and apply Gram–Schmidt to the columns of $P(x)U_0$. They remain linearly independent near $x_0$. This gives a $C^1$ matrix $U(x)$ with
\[
 U^*U=I_k,\qquad P=UU^*.
\]
Put
\[
 R=I-P,\qquad Z_\mu:=R\,\partial_\mu U.
\]
We claim that, at every point where the frame exists,
\[
 \boxed{\ \mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{Tr}(Z_\mu^*Z_\nu)\ }. \tag{1}
\]
Here is the complete calculation. Differentiating $P^2=P$ gives
$\partial_\mu P=P\partial_\mu P+(\partial_\mu P)P$, and hence
\[
 P(\partial_\mu P)P=0,\qquad R(\partial_\mu P)R=0. \tag{2}
\]
For $A=\partial_\mu P$ and $B=\partial_\nu P$, (2) yields
\[
 PAB=PARB=PARBP. \tag{3}
\]
Moreover, differentiating $P=UU^*$ gives
$\partial_\mu P=(\partial_\mu U)U^*+U(\partial_\mu U)^*$. Using $U^*R=0$ and $RU=0$, we obtain
\[
 PAR=U(\partial_\mu U)^*R,\qquad RBP=R(\partial_\nu U)U^*. \tag{4}
\]
Substitution of (4) into (3), followed by cyclicity and $U^*U=I_k$, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}
 &=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(U(\partial_\mu U)^*R
                 (\partial_\nu U)U^*\right)\\
 &=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left((\partial_\mu U)^*R\partial_\nu U\right)
 =\operatorname{Tr}(Z_\mu^*Z_\nu).
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (1). No horizontal or parallel gauge was chosen.

Equation (1) immediately gives
\[
 \mathcal Q_{\nu\mu}=\overline{\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}},\qquad
 g_{\mu\mu}=\|Z_\mu\|_F^2,\qquad
 g_{\nu\nu}=\|Z_\nu\|_F^2. \tag{5}
\]
For real coefficients $c_\mu$,
$\sum_{\mu\nu}c_\mu c_\nu g_{\mu\nu}
 =\|\sum_\mu c_\mu Z_\mu\|_F^2\ge0$, so $g$ is positive semidefinite.

For later comparison, the same projector identities show that the literal and projected tensors are related by
\[
 \boxed{\ Q^{\rm lit}_{\mu\nu}=2\operatorname{Re}\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu},
 \qquad g^{\rm lit}_{\mu\nu}=2g_{\mu\nu}.\ } \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, the off-diagonal decomposition supplied by (2) gives
$\partial_\mu P=PAR+RAP$ with $A=\partial_\mu P$, and similarly for $B$. Two orthogonal off-diagonal terms survive in $\operatorname{Tr}(AB)$; cyclicity and adjoints identify them as $\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}$ and its complex conjugate.

## 3. The sharp bound in every rank

Apply Cauchy–Schwarz in the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product to the two $n\times k$ matrices $Z_\mu,Z_\nu$:
\[
 |\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}|
 =|\operatorname{Tr}(Z_\mu^*Z_\nu)|
 \le \|Z_\mu\|_F\|Z_\nu\|_F
 =\sqrt{g_{\mu\mu}g_{\nu\nu}}.
\]
Since the modulus of the imaginary part is at most the modulus, we obtain
\[
 \boxed{\ |\operatorname{Im}\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}|
 \le \sqrt{g_{\mu\mu}g_{\nu\nu}}\ }. \tag{7}
\]
The constant is $1$, independently of $k$ and of $n$. In particular the written inequality with constant $2$ is also true for $\mathcal Q$, but is not optimal. For rank one, $U=v$ and (1) specializes to
$\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}=\langle R\partial_\mu v,R\partial_\nu v\rangle$, so no connection term has been silently discarded.

### Equality in (7)

If $Z_\mu=0$ or $Z_\nu=0$, both sides vanish. Otherwise equality holds if and only if both Cauchy–Schwarz and $|\operatorname{Im}\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}|=|\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}|$ are equalities. These two conditions are equivalent to
\[
 \boxed{\ Z_\nu=i r Z_\mu\quad\text{for some }r\in\mathbb R,
 \qquad |r|=\frac{\|Z_\nu\|_F}{\|Z_\mu\|_F}.\ } \tag{8}
\]
To see this, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $Z_\nu=\lambda Z_\mu$ for some $\lambda\in\mathbb C$, and then
$\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}=\lambda\|Z_\mu\|_F^2$. The condition
$\operatorname{Re}\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}=0$ says precisely that $\lambda$ is purely imaginary. The converse follows by direct substitution.

For the weaker inequality $|{}\operatorname{Im}\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}|
 \le2\sqrt{g_{\mu\mu}g_{\nu\nu}}$, equality together with (7) forces
$\sqrt{g_{\mu\mu}g_{\nu\nu}}=0$. Hence there can be no equality when both diagonal metric entries are positive. This disproves nondegenerate sharpness of the factor $2$ for $\operatorname{Im}\mathcal Q$.

## 4. Exact Berry-curvature identity and its sharp bound

For this subsection assume the frame (equivalently, locally, the projector family) is $C^2$, so that the connection below is $C^1$ and its exterior derivative is defined. The QGT inequality (7) itself needs only $C^1$.

Define the Berry connection matrix and its traced curvature by
\[
 \mathcal A_\mu:=iU^*\partial_\mu U,\qquad
 \mathcal F_{\mu\nu}:=
 \partial_\mu\operatorname{tr}\mathcal A_\nu-
 \partial_\nu\operatorname{tr}\mathcal A_\mu. \tag{9}
\]
This is the trace of the usual non-Abelian curvature; the trace of its commutator term is zero. With the opposite convention $-iU^*\partial_\mu U$, all displayed curvature signs are reversed and all modulus bounds are unchanged.

Taking derivatives in (9) and cancelling the terms containing second derivatives of $U$ gives
\[
 \mathcal F_{\mu\nu}
 =i\operatorname{Tr}\!\left((\partial_\mu U)^*\partial_\nu U
                         -(\partial_\nu U)^*\partial_\mu U\right). \tag{10}
\]
Let $C_\mu=U^*\partial_\mu U$. Differentiating $U^*U=I_k$ gives
$C_\mu^*+C_\mu=0$. Also
$\partial_\mu U=UC_\mu+Z_\mu$, and the two terms are Hilbert–Schmidt orthogonal because $U^*R=0$. Therefore, using (1),
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}((\partial_\mu U)^*\partial_\nu U)
 =\operatorname{tr}(C_\mu^*C_\nu)+\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}. \tag{11}
\]
The internal-connection terms cancel in the antisymmetric difference:
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(C_\mu^*C_\nu-C_\nu^*C_\mu)
 =-\operatorname{tr}(C_\mu C_\nu-C_\nu C_\mu)=0.
\]
Since $\mathcal Q_{\nu\mu}=\overline{\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}}$, (10) and (11) now yield
\[
 \boxed{\ \mathcal F_{\mu\nu}=-2\operatorname{Im}\mathcal Q_{\mu\nu}.\ } \tag{12}
\]
There is no additional rank factor. Combining (7) and (12) gives the sharp metric–curvature inequality
\[
 \boxed{\ |\mathcal F_{\mu\nu}|
 \le2\sqrt{g_{\mu\mu}g_{\nu\nu}}\ } \tag{13}
\]
in every rank. Equality is exactly the degenerate case or (8). The same equality condition therefore gives a calibrated complex-line condition for the two Hilbert–Schmidt tangent vectors.

If one insists on retaining the literal metric $g^{\rm lit}=2g$, then (6) and (13) equivalently say
\[
 |\mathcal F_{\mu\nu}|\le
 \sqrt{g^{\rm lit}_{\mu\mu}g^{\rm lit}_{\nu\nu}}.
\]
This normalization remark avoids a second, purely notational factor-of-$2$ ambiguity.

## 5. One smooth family attains equality in every rank

On $\mathbb C^2$ set
\[
 v(x,y)=\frac{(1,x+iy)^T}{\sqrt{1+x^2+y^2}},\qquad p(x,y)=vv^*.
\]
For $k>1$, append a fixed $(k-1)$-dimensional subspace and define
\[
 P(x,y)=p(x,y)\oplus I_{k-1}.
\]
For $k=1$, simply take $P=p$. This is a real-analytic rank-$k$ orthogonal-projector family near $(0,0)$ (in fact for all finite $x,y$).

At the origin $v=e_1$, $\partial_xv=e_2$, and $\partial_yv=i e_2$. These derivatives are transverse to $v$. The fixed summand has zero derivative. Thus the only nonzero column of $Z_x,Z_y$ is respectively $e_2,i e_2$, so $Z_y=iZ_x$ and
\[
 \mathcal Q(x,y)\big|_{(0,0)}
 =\begin{pmatrix}1&i\\-i&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}g_{xx}&g_{xy}\\g_{yx}&g_{yy}\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 |\operatorname{Im}\mathcal Q_{xy}|=1
 =\sqrt{g_{xx}g_{yy}},\qquad
 |\mathcal F_{xy}|=2=2\sqrt{g_{xx}g_{yy}},
\]
where the sign from (12) is $\mathcal F_{xy}=-2$. This proves sharpness of (7) and (13), including in ranks $k>1$.

At this same point $\partial_xP$ and $\partial_yP$ agree with Pauli matrices $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ on the moving $2\times2$ block (and vanish on the fixed block), giving
\[
 Q^{\rm lit}=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Hence the literal expression has a strict, nondegenerate gap
$0<2\sqrt{2\cdot2}=4$. For the projected tensor, the proposed inequality with constant $2$ likewise has the strict gap $1<2$. These are explicit counterexamples to the claim that either version is nondegenerately sharp with constant $2$; they are not counterexamples to the valid, but loose, one-sided inequality.

## 6. Corrected statement summary

With $g=\operatorname{Re}\mathcal Q$ and the Berry convention (9), the correct rank-independent table is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{quantity bounded} & \text{optimal constant }C&
 \text{bound }|X_{\mu\nu}|\le C\sqrt{g_{\mu\mu}g_{\nu\nu}}\\ \hline
 \operatorname{Im}Q^{\rm lit} & 0 & 0=0\\
 \operatorname{Im}\mathcal Q & 1 & \text{sharp, all ranks}\\
 \mathcal F & 2 & \text{sharp, all ranks}
\end{array}
\]
Thus no extra rank-dependent constant and no counterexample to a higher-rank inequality are needed: the Hilbert–Schmidt Gram proof already covers every $k$.
